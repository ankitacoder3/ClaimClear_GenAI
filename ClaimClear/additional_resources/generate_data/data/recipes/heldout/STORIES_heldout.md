# Held-out case stories (40 cases, 16 scenarios: H1-H16)

Held-out cases are locked and never used to tune the system.
All people, hospitals and insurers are fictional. Policy wording: IFFCO Tokio Arogya Sanjeevani, issued as
**Narmada Health & General Insurance Ltd.** Each scenario has 2 or 3 variants.


---

## H1 · Appendicitis in a student

A 19-year-old college student from Kochi with a ₹1 lakh policy since 2023 is rushed in with sudden pain low on
the right of his abdomen. It is acute appendicitis, removed by keyhole surgery. His family picks a twin-sharing
room at ₹3,000 a day, above his ₹2,000 limit, for three days. The hospital settles cashless.

1. **Correct settlement (complex).** He arrives by road ambulance (₹2,600). Room capped at ₹2,000, other hospital
   charges cut in proportion with medicines spared, ambulance capped at ₹2,000, crepe bandage (not payable) and
   documentation charges (part of room charges) removed, co-pay taken. Every deduction is right.
2. **Proportionate deduction.** Narmada also cuts the medicines in proportion.
3. **Document inconsistency.** The bill's printed grand total is ₹1,500 more than its own lines add up to.
   Narmada settles on the lines; the system must flag that the bill disagrees with itself.

## H2 · Knee replacement

A 62-year-old woman from Ahmedabad with a ₹5 lakh policy and a 15% cumulative bonus has her left knee replaced
for arthritis, staying five days in a private room. Joint replacement has a 48-month waiting period, and she has
had cover since July 2022. The bill includes the implant, a knee immobiliser and a walker. Cashless.

1. **Correct settlement (complex).** Room at exactly her ₹5,000 limit, so no cut. Her cover is 50 months old, just
   past the 48-month waiting period. Implant paid in full; immobiliser and walker removed as not payable; the 15%
   bonus counted; co-pay taken. Every deduction is right.
2. **Room / ICU cap.** Her sum insured is ₹4 lakh and the room costs ₹6,000. The limit is ₹5,000 a day (2%,
   capped at ₹5,000), but Narmada uses 1% of the sum insured (₹4,000), cutting the room and everything in
   proportion too far.
3. **Co-pay.** Narmada takes the 5% co-pay on the whole bill instead of on the admitted amount.

## H3 · Laser prostate surgery (modern treatment)

A 69-year-old man from Chennai with a ₹2 lakh policy since 2022 has an enlarged prostate treated with holmium
laser vaporisation, one of the "modern treatments" the policy caps at half the sum insured (₹1 lakh). He stays
two days in a semi-private room within his limit. The bill is about ₹1.4 lakh. Cashless.

1. **Correct settlement (complex).** He also had an unrelated claim for a urine infection earlier this year
   (letter included); that does not touch the modern-treatment limit and enough sum insured is left.
   Registration charges removed, claim limited to ₹1 lakh, co-pay taken. Every deduction is right.
2. **Sub-limit.** Narmada caps modern treatment at a quarter of the sum insured (₹50,000) instead of half.
3. **Not verifiable.** The letter says part of his modern-treatment limit was already used by an earlier claim,
   but no earlier settlement letter is provided and nothing else shows it.

## H4 · Typhoid soon after buying the policy

A 24-year-old teacher from Indore buys her first ₹1.5 lakh policy in late August 2026 and is admitted with
typhoid for four days in the general ward. The bill includes mineral water, administrative and
registration charges. She claims reimbursement.

1. **Correct settlement (correct rejection).** She is admitted on day 25 of cover. Typhoid is an illness, not an
   accident, so the first-30-days exclusion applies and Narmada rightly rejects the claim. The system should
   confirm the rejection, not dispute it.
2. **Waiting period.** She is admitted on day 38, but Narmada still rejects the claim under the first-30-days rule.
3. **Non-payable.** Admitted on day 38, but Narmada refuses all the blood tests as "admission for investigation only". They were part of
   her treatment.

## H5 · Tonsil surgery for a child on a floater

An 8-year-old girl from Mysuru is covered on her parents' ₹3 lakh family floater; she was added 30 months ago.
After repeated throat infections her tonsils are removed. Her mother picks a single room at ₹6,500 a day, above
the ₹5,000 limit, for one night. Tonsillectomy has a 24-month waiting period. Reimbursement.

1. **Proportionate deduction.** Narmada works out the proportion using a ₹7,500 "suite" rate instead of the
   ₹6,500 room on the bill, cutting every charge too far.
2. **Waiting period.** Narmada rejects the claim by counting the 24 months from the family's latest renewal
   instead of the date the child was first covered.

## H6 · Irregular heartbeat in a retired teacher

A 66-year-old retired teacher from Vadodara with a ₹3 lakh policy since 2020 is admitted with a fast, irregular
heartbeat (atrial fibrillation). She spends two days in the cardiac ICU at ₹9,000 a day, within the ₹10,000 ICU
limit, then two days in a ward. Cashless.

1. **Room / ICU cap.** Narmada caps the ICU days at the ordinary room limit (₹5,000) instead of the ICU limit.
2. **Co-pay.** Narmada takes a 10% co-pay because she is over 60. Arogya Sanjeevani has 5% for everyone.

## H7 · Second admission in one year for jaundice

A 47-year-old shopkeeper from Patna with a ₹2 lakh policy since 2021 was admitted in April with hepatitis;
Narmada paid ₹1,20,000 of a ₹1,45,000 bill (letter included). In October he is admitted again for five days with
jaundice. The new bill is within what is left of his sum insured. Reimbursement.

1. **Sub-limit.** Narmada works out what is left of his sum insured from the April amount **billed** instead of
   the amount **paid**, and pays short.
2. **Not verifiable.** The letter cuts ₹3,800 "as per tariff agreement", which cannot be checked from the documents.

## H8 · Burns from a kitchen accident

A 35-year-old woman from Ranchi buys a ₹2 lakh policy, and three weeks later is burned by hot oil while cooking.
She stays four days in a single room at ₹5,500, above her ₹4,000 limit, for dressings and IV fluids. Burns from an
accident are covered from day one. Reimbursement.

1. **Proportionate deduction.** Narmada caps the room rent and then cuts the capped rent again in proportion.
2. **Non-payable.** Narmada refuses the burn dressings as "crepe bandage, List I". They are medicated burn
   dressings, part of her treatment and payable.

## H9 · Road accident fracture

A 34-year-old delivery rider from Jaipur buys his first ₹3 lakh policy, and 16 days later a car hits him. His
shin bone is fixed with a plate and screws; he arrives by road ambulance (₹3,500). The first-30-days waiting
period does not apply to accidents. Reimbursement.

1. **Correct settlement.** Semi-private room within limit; ambulance capped at ₹2,000, crepe bandage and splint
   removed, co-pay taken.
2. **Room / ICU cap.** He takes a ₹6,000 single room for four days; Narmada deducts the excess rent for five days.
3. **Waiting period.** The bill clearly records a road traffic accident, but Narmada rejects the claim under the
   first-30-days rule, which does not apply to accidents.

## H10 · Angioplasty after an earlier heart claim

A 58-year-old man from Kolkata with a ₹4 lakh policy since 2020 had an angiography earlier this year (letter
included). Now he has an angioplasty with one stent: two days in the cardiac ICU and two in a single room, both
within limits. Even after the earlier claim, enough sum insured is left to pay in full. Cashless.

1. **Correct settlement.** ECG electrodes removed, co-pay taken, paid in full.
2. **Sub-limit.** Narmada works out what is left of his sum insured from the amount billed on the earlier claim
   instead of the amount paid, and pays short.
3. **Sub-limit.** He has a 10% cumulative bonus, which adds to his sum insured. Narmada leaves it out and pays short.

## H11 · Hysterectomy on a family floater

A 44-year-old woman from Bhopal, covered with her husband and two children on a ₹3 lakh family floater since 2022,
has her womb removed by keyhole surgery for fibroids. She stays three days in a private room at ₹5,500, above
her ₹5,000 limit. Reimbursement.

1. **Correct settlement (complex).** She also spends the night after surgery in the high-dependency unit at
   ₹12,500, above the ₹10,000 ICU limit. Both are capped, and because the ICU ratio (10,000/12,500) is lower than
   the room ratio (5,000/5,500), the ICU ratio is applied to the other charges, medicines spared. Gauze (part of
   procedure charges) and sanitary pads (not payable) removed, co-pay taken. Every deduction is right.
2. **Proportionate deduction.** Narmada caps the room rent and then cuts the capped rent again in proportion.
3. **Co-pay.** Narmada takes a 20% co-pay "for a non-network hospital". Arogya Sanjeevani has no such rule.

## H12 · Hernia repair with a day-wise bill

A 52-year-old factory supervisor from Coimbatore with a ₹2.5 lakh policy and a 20% cumulative bonus, covered
since 2022, has a groin hernia repaired by keyhole surgery with a mesh. The hospital gives a day-wise bill. Cashless.

1. **Correct settlement (complex).** Two days in a ₹5,000 room, exactly at his limit. Two dressing visits after
   discharge (days 6 and 15) are paid as post-hospitalisation; a pharmacy refill on day 70 is correctly refused as
   outside 60 days. Abdominal binder (not payable) and discharge charges (part of room charges) removed, 20% bonus
   counted, co-pay taken. Every deduction is right.
2. **Proportionate deduction.** He takes a ₹6,000 room; Narmada works out the proportion with a ₹4,000 limit
   instead of ₹5,000.
3. **Non-payable.** Narmada refuses the mesh as a "surgical consumable". It is an implant used in the surgery and payable.

## H13 · Pneumonia in a toddler

A 3-year-old boy from Guwahati on his parents' ₹2 lakh floater (covered since birth) is admitted with pneumonia
for four days. His parents choose a ₹5,000 single room, above the ₹4,000 limit. Cashless.

1. **Proportionate deduction.** Narmada also cuts the medicines (antibiotics and nebulised drugs) in proportion.
2. **Document inconsistency.** The settlement letter gives an admission date a year earlier than the bill, before
   the current policy period began. The documents disagree; this must be flagged.

## H14 · Sinus surgery

A 45-year-old accountant from Lucknow with a ₹3 lakh policy since 2022 has long-standing sinus blockage cleared
by endoscopic sinus surgery, one night in a semi-private room within his limit. Sinus disorders have a 24-month
waiting period. Reimbursement.

1. **Waiting period.** Narmada rejects the claim saying sinus surgery has a 48-month waiting period. It is on the
   24-month list, which he has passed.
2. **Co-pay.** Narmada takes the 5% co-pay on the whole bill instead of on the admitted amount.

## H15 · Stroke in an older man

A 64-year-old retired bus driver from Nagpur with a ₹5 lakh policy since 2019 has a stroke. He spends four days
in the neuro ICU at ₹9,500 a day, within the ₹10,000 limit, then three days in a ward. Cashless.

1. **Room / ICU cap.** The bill shows four ICU days, but Narmada pays ICU rent for only two.
2. **Not verifiable.** The letter shows a lump sum of "other deductions as per policy terms" with no breakup.

## H16 · Eye injections for diabetic retinopathy

A 59-year-old woman from Madurai with a ₹3 lakh policy since 2020 receives an injection into her right eye for
diabetic swelling of the retina, as day care. Intravitreal injections are a "modern treatment" (half the sum
insured), and she has had two earlier injections this year (letters included). Reimbursement.

1. **Document inconsistency.** The bill dates the injection one day after the discharge date printed on the same bill.
2. **Non-payable.** Narmada refuses the injected drug as "not a medicine". It is the treatment itself and payable.
